01 · First-Time User Orientation

How TreeviewEA Works: Repository Reuse, 10 Frameworks & the 6×6 Matrix

When you open TreeviewEA after installing the macOS .dmg, you are working inside a true repository-based modeling studio. Unlike drawing tools where every box is just a shape on a page, every element you create in TreeviewEA is saved in your .tvwx project repository as a reusable Architecture Element so you can place it across 1...M Architecture Diagrams, attach Class Attributes, Table Columns, WBS hours, CMMI maturity ratings, and security controls, and trace your architecture from the boardroom to the cloud.

STEP 1 · PICK YOUR FRAMEWORKS

Click 🏛️ EACOE or Use the Left Toolbox

Open the 🏛️ EACOE & Frameworks workspace (or toggle the Frameworks palette in the Modeling Studio) to instantiate ready-made Packages / Model Folders, starter Architecture Diagrams, and elements for Gartner EA, BizBOK® v14, SABSA® 6×6, NIST CSF 2.0 & SP 800-53, BIAN v12, TOGAF® 10, ArchiMate® 3.2, ISO 20022, Value Innovation & Market Space Strategy (value_innovation), or CMMI® v3.0 (cmmi).

STEP 2 · MODEL ONCE, REUSE EVERYWHERE

Drag Canonical Elements Across 1...M Diagrams

Create a «StrategyCanvas», «Capability», «Microservice», TransactionPlatformObject code class, or «Table» database table once, then drag it from the Project Browser onto your Strategy Canvas, Capability Map, Sequence Diagram, Database ERD, and Cloud Deployment Diagram. Rename it once and all diagrams update automatically.

STEP 3 · VALIDATE & PLAN DELIVERY

Score PACE/TIME, PMS, CMMI, WBS & Publish

Use the Right Inspector and the 🛡️ Governance & 📊 Analytics workspaces to classify components by Gartner PACE/TIME and Value Innovation PMS (`Pioneer`, `Migrator`, `Settler`), appraise CMMI Maturity Levels 1–5, assign team rate cards and WBS hours, verify SABSA/NIST security coverage, and publish interactive HTML or multi-page PDF architecture books.

AUTO TAXONOMY NUMBERING + OVERRIDE

Hierarchical IDs (CAP-1.1 → CAP-1.1.1) with ↻ Auto Reset

Every element across all 10 frameworks (CAP-1.1 → CAP-1.1.1, VS-1.1, OUT-1.1, SC-1.1, ERRC-1.1, PATH-1.1, UTIL-1.1, PRC-1.1, PMS-1.1, NC-1.1, SEQ-1.1, CMMI-3.1, SABSA-2.1, NIST-2.1, BIAN-2.1, ISO-3.1, APP-2.1, CLS-3.1, TBL-3.1, INF-4.1, REQ-1.1) is automatically assigned a hierarchical taxonomy number based on its package and parent-child containment (1.0 → 1.1 → 1.1.1). 03 · Multi-Framework Interoperability

10-Framework Metamodel Cross-Walk & Hierarchy Matrix (L0–L4 × R1–R6)

In TreeviewEA, frameworks are never mutually exclusive silos. You can mix and match any of the 10 supported optional frameworks inside a single project—for example, using Value Innovation & Market Space Strategy (value_innovation) for uncontested market space formulation, BizBOK® v14 and BIAN v12 for business capabilities, Gartner EA (PACE & TIME) for application portfolio strategy, CMMI® v3.0 (cmmi) for engineering & process maturity, ISO 20022 for canonical financial messages, and SABSA® 6×6 + NIST CSF 2.0 / SP 800-53 for end-to-end security and risk assurance.

Deep Dive: The 9 Analytical Tools of Value Innovation & Market Space Strategy (value_innovation)

The Value Innovation & Market Space Strategy (value_innovation) framework provides 9 generic diagnostic and action-oriented analytical tools to shift architecture investments from Contested Market Space into Uncontested Market Space by simultaneously pursuing differentiation and low cost:

All 9 Core Diagnostic & Action-Oriented Analytical Tools in the Value Innovation & Market Space Strategy Framework
Analytical Tool Built-In Stereotypes Layer & 6×6 Cell Strategic Purpose How It Works & Connects Across L0–L4
1. The Strategy Canvas «StrategyCanvas», «CompetitiveFactor», «ValueCurve» L0 (R1:C1, R1:C6) Diagnostic tool and action framework to capture the current state of play in an existing market space and visualize an organization's strategic profile. Maps the industry's competitive factors («CompetitiveFactor») on the horizontal axis and the level of offering customers receive on the vertical axis. The resulting «ValueCurve» highlights how far an organization's strategy diverges from competitors into uncontested market space.
2. The Four Actions Framework «FourActionsFramework», «EliminateFactor», «ReduceFactor», «RaiseFactor», «CreateFactor» L1 (R2:C2, R2:C6) Reconstructs buyer value elements and creates a new value curve that simultaneously pursues differentiation and low cost (value innovation). Challenges industry assumptions by asking four key questions: 1. Eliminate («EliminateFactor»: Which factors taken for granted should be eliminated?), 2. Reduce («ReduceFactor»: Which factors should be reduced well below industry standards?), 3. Raise («RaiseFactor»: Which factors should be raised well above industry standards?), 4. Create («CreateFactor»: What factors should be created that the industry has never offered?).
3. The ERRC Grid (Eliminate-Reduce-Raise-Create Grid) «ERRCGrid» L1 → L2/L3 (R2:C2 → R3:C2) A practical matrix that brings the Four Actions Framework into a concrete, actionable workspace. Teams fill out a four-box «ERRCGrid» based on the Four Actions Framework, forcing the organization to drop obsolete or costly features/systems (linking «EliminateFactor» to Gartner «EliminateAsset») while introducing new sources of value (linking «CreateFactor» to «PioneerOffering» & «SystemOfInnovation») rather than copying rivals.
4. The Six Paths Framework (Six Market Boundaries) «SixPathsOpportunity», «AlternativeIndustryPath», «StrategicGroupPath», «BuyerChainPath», «ComplementaryOfferingPath», «FunctionalEmotionalPath», «ExternalTrendPath» L0–L1 (R1:C3, R2:C3, R2:C4) Helps organizations systematically look across alternative market boundaries to discover commercially viable uncontested market opportunities. Guides strategists beyond standard industry boundaries by examining: 1. Alternative industries, 2. Strategic groups within industries, 3. The chain of buyers (purchasers, users, influencers), 4. Complementary product and service offerings, 5. Functional-or-emotional appeal to buyers, and 6. Time (shaping external trends rather than just adapting to them).
5. The Buyer Utility Map «BuyerUtilityMap», «BuyerExperienceStage», «UtilityLever», «UtilityBlock» L1 (R2:C1, R2:C5) Evaluates how much utility an offering truly delivers to customers across their entire experience cycle. Maps the 6 stages of the buyer's experience cycle (Purchase, Delivery, Use, Supplements, Maintenance, Disposal) against 6 levers of customer utility (Customer Productivity, Simplicity, Convenience, Risk Reduction, Fun/Image, Environmental Friendliness) to spot blocks to utility («UtilityBlock») and uncover new utility spaces.
6. The Price Corridor of the Mass «PriceCorridorOfTheMass», «StrategicPricePoint» L1 (R2:C1, R2:C6) Helps organizations determine the right pricing strategy to capture the absolute mass of target buyers, rather than just a niche segment. Guides businesses to identify the price bandwidth and strategic price point («StrategicPricePoint») that attracts masses of buyers from both within the industry and from alternative, different-form industries offering the same function.
7. The Pioneer-Migrator-Settler (PMS) Map «PMSPortfolioMap», «PioneerOffering», «MigratorOffering», «SettlerOffering» L2 (R3:C1, R3:C2) Evaluates and plans an organization's portfolio matrix based on its future growth and innovation potential. Classifies current and planned offerings into: Settlers («SettlerOffering», value-imitation / me-too offerings), Migrators («MigratorOffering», value-improvers that are better but not breakthrough), and Pioneers («PioneerOffering», value-innovators offering unprecedented buyer value in uncontested market space).
8. The Three Tiers of Noncustomers «NoncustomerTier1», «NoncustomerTier2», «NoncustomerTier3» L0 (R1:C4) Broadens the target market scope by helping organizations look beyond existing customers to unlock massive untapped demand. Categorizes noncustomers into: Tier 1 («NoncustomerTier1»: Soon-to-be noncustomers who minimally use current market offerings and are ready to jump ship), Tier 2 («NoncustomerTier2»: Refusing noncustomers who consciously reject the industry's offerings), and Tier 3 («NoncustomerTier3»: Unexplored noncustomers who have never been targeted or considered by the industry).
9. The Value Innovation Viability Sequence «ViabilitySequenceGate», «ExceptionalBuyerUtility», «StrategicPriceGate», «TargetCostStructure», «AdoptionHurdleMitigation» L0 → L3 (R1:C5 → R5:C6) Ensures commercial viability and risk reduction of a value-innovation initiative before execution. Establishes a strict order of gates that must be cleared: 1. Exceptional Buyer Utility («ExceptionalBuyerUtility») → 2. Strategic Price («StrategicPriceGate», accessible to the mass) → 3. Target Cost («TargetCostStructure», profitable at that strategic price) → 4. Overcoming Adoption Hurdles («AdoptionHurdleMitigation», managing employee, partner, or stakeholder resistance).
